About

I design quantum error-correcting codes that are high-rate, single-shot decodable and ready for real hardware.

I'm a PhD student in the Manning College of Information and Computer Sciences at UMass Amherst, advised by Stefan Krastanov, and I collaborate with Owen Gwilliam in Mathematics.

My work builds quantum LDPC codes from algebra, using Koszul complexes, group algebras and Cayley complexes. I test them at practical sizes with circuit-level simulations in Stim and modern decoders. I also lead development of the error-correcting-code library in QuantumClifford.jl, so the codes in my papers can be built in a few lines of Julia.

Before quantum error correction I trained as an electrical engineer in Lahore, building FPGA and system-on-chip accelerators. I'm open to research internships in quantum error correction and fault-tolerant architectures; email me.
